## Part 1 — Wave 22's four carried riders

### 1. W22-R3-2 — the frozen-column cap

**The guard bites, and bites wider than 29.** On a scratch copy with the
`max-width: min(62vw, 20rem)` line removed from `app.css` (leaving
`white-space: normal; overflow-wrap: anywhere;` in place), `verify.mjs`'s
sticky-column check now names **33** tables with a frozen column wider than
its pane at 375px (worst: `#statistics` at 1801px inside a 306px pane) — more
than the "29" Wave 22 closed on, because Wave 23 added `#map` tables that
themselves overflow uncapped (`The 14 layers §5.2 names` alone hits 1052px in
a 306px pane). The rise from 29 to 33 is new tables, not a regression in the
fix.

**The cap cannot leak to desktop, and I measured rather than trusted the
media-query boundary.** At 1280px, across every `.sf-table--matrix` identity
cell on every screen (1092 cells measured), `getComputedStyle(th).maxWidth`
is `none` in every single case — the `@media (max-width: 1119px)` scope holds
structurally, not just by inspection. Desktop-width cells *do* wrap in
places (445 of 1092), but this is pre-existing: `git show 2c24741^:app.css`
shows `.sf-table--matrix { min-width: 100% }` at the `1120px` breakpoint
already existed **before** Wave 22 touched the file — the desktop wrap comes
from ordinary `table-layout: auto` squeezing inside `min-width: 100%`, a
mechanism the cap cannot have introduced because the cap's rule is provably
inert up there. **Confirmed: the cap did not make any desktop column wrap
where there was room.**

### 2. W22-R3-1 — the refusal-in-label buttons

### 4. The readability judgment — is the cap the right fix?

I measured this myself rather than trusting the figures handed to me, and my
own numbers are close to, but not identical to, the ones quoted in the task.
Comparing the capped build against the same build with the cap removed, at
375px, across the 33 (now-)overflowing tables:

| | 62vw (shipped) | 45vw (alternative) | Uncapped (pre-Wave-22) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frozen-column width | 233px | 169px | up to 1801px |
| Slot left in a 306px pane for the rest of the table | **73px** | **137px** | **0px reachable at any scroll offset** |
| Worst identity-cell wrap | **18 line-boxes** (`#statistics`) | 23 line-boxes (same table) | 3 lines (unwrapped) |
| Table-height inflation vs. uncapped | up to **2.46×** (`#notification`, not 2.30× — the worst case is notification, not statistics) | — | 1.0× (baseline) |
| Height cost of 45vw over 62vw | — | **+24% to +41%** across the 15 worst tables (not quite the quoted 28–46% ceiling, but the same shape) | — |

The 398px-wide second column read through a 74px slot on `#notification` is
real and I reproduced it to the pixel: `pane=306px`, frozen column
`233px`, second column (`Instrument`) `398px` — `306 − 233 = 73px`, one pixel
off "74" from rounding. At 45vw the same row's slot becomes `306 − 169 =
137px`, exactly as claimed, at the cost of +37.2% table height on that one
table (my number; the range across tables is 24–41%, not quite the quoted
46% ceiling but the same order).

**Verdict: the shipped 62vw cap is the lesser evil, not the right fix, and
neither number is close to right for the worst 5–8 tables.** For the ~25
tables whose identity column is a short code (`MW01A`, `IMP-0243`), the whole
question is moot — those wrap to 2–4 lines under either value and the cap
never mattered. For the handful whose identity column is prose (a statute
name, a §6.2/§6.3 item description, a population label), *every* vw-based
cap trades vertical bloat against horizontal slot width without touching the
actual cause: a matrix/crosstab layout — one frozen short-label column beside
scrollable data columns — applied to a table whose "identity" is a sentence
rather than a code. 62vw is the better default of the two because an
802px-tall single cell (45vw's worst case) costs more reading time than a
73px slot a reader pans through one column at a time, which is a bounded,
familiar mobile gesture. But the right fix is neither number: the 5–8 outlier
tables want a different narrow-viewport pattern (a stacked card/definition
list, or a truncate-with-expand on the identity cell) rather than a global
`vw` formula applied uniformly to every `.sf-table--matrix`. This is filed as
**P2** below — a real, now-quantified cost, disclosed by the wave rather than
hidden, that the wave correctly declined to spend more effort chasing inside
its own scope.

### 4. Selection produces a population — every number reproduced independently

**The projection arithmetic, from hand-retyped literals, in Python (not
imported from the codebase's own `POTENTIOMETRIC_FIT`).** Retyped the 7
`HEAD_CONTROL` points' coordinates and heads (computing `h = toc − depthToWater`
for the 5 reduced ones from `LOCATIONS`/`FIELD_ROUND` literals, keeping
STY01/STY02's estimated heads as given), solved the least-squares plane by
Cramer's rule (a different method from the codebase's Gaussian elimination),
and got:

```
bearing: 102.84° → rounds to 103°
gradient: 1.7365 m/km → rounds to 1.74 m/km
worst residual: 0.549 m at MW01A → rounds to 0.55 m
TP01 along-flow distance from TSF-VWP-03: −91.79 m → rounds to 92 m upgradient
```

All four match the codebase's own `POTENTIOMETRIC_FIT` export exactly
(cross-checked: `bearingText: 103°`, `gradientText: 1.74 m/km`, `worstText:

TP01 does project 92 m upgradient.** The on-screen table shows this as
`−0.09` km (2-decimal precision), which is the same −91.79 m rounded to
kilometres rather than metres — not a discrepancy, a different unit.

### 5. Non-detect treatment — six cells confirmed exact, but the four verdicts are typed, not arithmetic (P1)

**The six cells.** `CROSSTAB`'s cadmium row, filtered for `cell.censored`,
gives exactly `[MW01A, MW03B, MW05, MW07, MW09, MW12]` — the identical set,
same order, as `INDETERMINATE.filter(i => i.analyte === 'Cadmium

session. **Exactly INDETERMINATE's six rows, as claimed.**

**The four rule outcomes are not arithmetic.** `SPATIAL`'s `nonDetectRules`
array types the verdict word for each rule (`reads: 'exceedance'`,
`reads: 'compliant'`, …) and the qualitative wording in each `says` sentence
("is above", "is below") as fixed strings — only the *numbers* interpolated
into those sentences (`cadmiumLimit`, `cadmiumCriterion`,
`cadmiumLimit / 2`) are computed live. I mutated the seed's cadmium criterion
from `a: '0.54'` to `a: '0.3'` (analytically flipping which side of 0.3 the
half-LOR substitution of 0.5 falls on) on a scratch copy. `node build.mjs`
still exits **0** — no guard catches it — and the built page now reads:

> *"0.5 is below 0.3, so the same six bores would be coloured as compliant"*

which is false (0.5 > 0.3), sitting directly beside the correctly-updated
numbers, with the status badge still reading **compliant** when the
arithmetic it claims to state now says **exceedance**. On the shipped
(unmutated) data the page is truthful — this is not a live falsehood — but
it is exactly the fragile-derivation shape this codebase has a name for and
nine prior instances of: a value that could trivially be a one-line ternary
(`cadmiumLimit / 2 > cadmiumCriterion ? 'exceedance' : 'compliant'`) is
instead asserted, and the assertion happens to match today's numbers. The
`#coverage` row for §5.3 explicitly claims *"The non-detect treatment … is
answered with arithmetic"* — a claim the record does not fully support: the
*counts* are arithmetic, the *four verdicts and their wording* are not.
**Filed as P1** (below) because it is a specific, named, on-the-record claim
("answered with arithmetic") that a direct mutation shows to be only half
true, on the one panel the wave itself flags as "the one that must not be
skipped."

## Part 3 — Reading `#map` as a practitioner

Walked the built `#map` screen top to bottom at 1280px and 375px (screenshots
taken at both widths; the screen is 13,473px tall at 1280 and 21,397px at
375 — long, but a monitoring-network workspace with 14 governed layers, 7
symbology dimensions and a location-investigation panel is inherently a lot
of screen).

**What actually works as investigation, not inventory:**
- The selection section (§5.6) is a real workflow, not a description of one:
  it states a rule ("along the flow direction… from TSF-VWP-03"), shows the
  disagreement between three ways of asking the same question with the
  actual bore codes named on both sides, and hands a population to
  `#explorer` through the same counting function the receiving screen uses.
  A practitioner reading this would understand *why* MW09 and MW11 are
  contested (typed as "Compliance boundary," a role, not a position) rather
  than just being told they are.
- The "MW05 — the panel, drawn as it opens" section genuinely demonstrates
  in-map investigation: nine reach-through links (construction, hydrograph,
  crosstab, QA/QC, documents…) beside the map rather than instead of it, and
  it contrasts MW05's 9-of-9 against a decommissioned bore's 1-of-9 to make
  the *point* of the panel legible, not just its mechanics.
- The non-detect four-rule table is the single most valuable thing on the
  screen for a practitioner specifically, because it names a real,
  consequential ambiguity (a censored cadmium result reads compliant,
  exceedance, absent or indeterminate depending on an invisible rule) that
  every incumbent this product competes with gets wrong silently.

**What still reads as inventory rather than workspace:**
- Of §5.2's 14 layers, only 5 are drawn and all 5 share one undifferentiated
  plate — a practitioner cannot toggle "just the surface-water points" and
  see the network without pits and bores, because that toggle does not
  animate anything (the "Active layers" checkboxes are static, and say so).
  The screen is honest about this ("Toggling a layer is a view control and
  writes nothing") but honesty about a limitation does not make the
  limitation go away: a hydrogeologist who actually needs to isolate
  bores-only, for instance to eyeball flow direction without soil-pit
  clutter, cannot do it here.
- The symbology section is a table describing what *could* be coloured
  rather than a control that colours anything — the bar's five dropdowns
  are wired to nothing; changing "Colour by" from exceedance to elevation
  does not repaint Figure 4.9, because it cannot (the figure is one static
  SVG). This is disclosed plainly ("the plate is not redrawn under it") and
  is the correct call under D13's fence — but it means the screen's actual
  *interactive surface* is zero controls, however well-measured the
  narrative around them is.
- Seven of fourteen layers are pure prose rows with no visual anchor at
  all — a practitioner scanning for "where's geology" finds a paragraph
  explaining why there is no geology, which is honest and useful the first
  time and becomes a wall of near-identical "nothing holds it / cannot be
  stated" cells on a second or third visit.

**Verdict on Part 3.** `#map` answers §5's question — *can somebody
investigate here* — for exactly the two things Wave 23 actually built:
spatial selection with hand-off, and per-location reach-through. Those two
are genuine, load-bearing workspace behaviour, independently verified above
(the arithmetic is real, the accounting is shared, the panel reaches rather
than restates). For the other four fifths of the brief's ask — the layers
themselves, and symbolising by them — the screen is not theatre (it never
claims to draw what it does not), but it is also not yet a workspace: it is
a rigorously accurate, well-governed *description* of a workspace that does
not exist yet, sitting beside the two small pieces of one that do. A
practitioner would leave this screen understanding precisely what Strataflow
cannot yet show them about their site, which is valuable and honest, and
would leave still unable to look at their network coloured by trend or
filtered to soil locations only. That is the correct state for a wave fenced
by D13 to be in — but it means "the map becomes a workspace" is the right
name for the destination and an overstatement of what shipped, and the
screen's own words are more careful than the wave's title.
