## Part 3 — the closing judgement

### 1. Is the catalogue's central claim defensible as stated?

**No, not as an unqualified, catalogue-wide statement — measured twice now,
independently, at 50% and 47%.** "Every number on every face is computed
from the register that owns it" reads as a universal claim and is checked,
by direct random sampling, at roughly a coin flip. That is not a rounding
error against "every."

But the claim is not *uniformly* false either, and the two samples say
something more specific than "half is wrong": every screen this audit chain
has actually spent a round on — `#map` (twice), `#crosstab`, `#qc`'s own
count, `#consistency` — comes back clean under mutation, because mutation
testing is exactly what finds and fixes this shape of defect, and those
screens have had it applied. The screens nobody has looked at closely yet —
ordinary CRUD-adjacent registers, import stats, instrument logs, certificate
facts — sit at roughly 50/50, not because they are badly built (13 of 25
misses across both samples are demonstrably, currently correct against a
real register) but because nobody has gone through and swapped the literal
for the expression the register already supports.

**Proposed wording**, in the same register-and-lineage voice the rest of the
catalogue uses:

> *"Every number this audit chain has tested — the ones behind a mutation,
> not the ones merely read — is computed from the register that owns it.
> Two independent random samples of the rest, 25 numbers across 23 screens,
> found the same rate twice: roughly half derive live and half are typed
> literals, most of them currently correct against a register already on the
> page and not yet reading from it."*

That is a claim the record actually supports, names its own limit rather
than asserting past it, and gives a future auditor a number to try to move
rather than a universal to take on faith — which is exactly the standard
this catalogue holds every other screen's own prose to. The current wording
should be retired; it is precisely the "typed claim beside live numbers"
shape this whole audit series exists to catch, now found on the catalogue's
own summary of itself.

### 2. What is the single most important thing still wrong — not with Wave 23, with the whole body of work?

**There is no guard for the one defect shape that recurs the most, even
though this catalogue has guards for almost everything structurally
adjacent to it.** `build.mjs` and `verify.mjs` together catch: a table naming
a count it does not have, a duplicate DOM id, unrendered markdown emphasis, a
figure `<desc>` no longer attached to the plate it is quoted for, a banned
state-claim phrase on the wrong kind of screen, a population reading whose
array literal drifted. Every one of those is a mechanised answer to "does
this claim still match the thing it claims to describe."

The one shape with no such guard is the one this session found nine
additional live instances of in a single afternoon of grepping: **a
qualitative relationship between two numbers, typed as prose, standing next
to the numbers themselves, which are live.** W23-1 was the tenth recorded
instance of it (the commit message's own count). W23-R2-1 was an eleventh,
found by following the same two numbers three sentences further than the
first fix reached. This round's Part 1 finds a twelfth through
eighteenth — on `#qc`, `#result-detail`, `#hardness`, `#indeterminate`
(three separate sentences), `#dqa` and `#exceedances` — in the time it took
to grep for "sits above" and "sits below." Two of those eighteen instances
(`#qc`'s `rule.says`, `#dqa`'s table cell) go further and explicitly claim
*"the comparison is arithmetic"* while being exactly the opposite.

Fixing each instance as it is found, one wave and one round at a time, chases
the symptom. The pattern is regular enough to be caught mechanically: a
sentence containing a comparison word (*above/below/exceeds/sits*) adjacent
to two numeric literals that also exist as named fields in the seed is
exactly the shape `build.mjs`'s existing checks already flag *other*
staleness patterns for. A guard here would not need to be perfect — even a
noisy one that lists every candidate sentence for a human to clear, the way
the fixture-guard and glossary-guard already work by exact-match rather than
semantic understanding, would have caught all eighteen instances found so
far without anyone having to think to grep for them again. That guard not
existing — after the tenth, eleventh and now an eighteenth instance of the
identical shape — is the single most important thing still wrong with this
body of work.

### 3. What should the owner do first?

**Order: the print test, then the second practitioner walk, then the PRD
amendment.**

1. **The print test first, and it is not close.** It is the one item of the
   three that is purely blocking rather than merely due. `figures.mjs` has
   been fenced since D13, self-approved and never printed, and this wave's
   own response says so twice: "This is the strongest argument in the
   catalogue for running the print test, and it is now the second wave to
   say so." Everything Part 3's own prompt names as still wrong with `#map`
   — the symbology bar wired to nothing, seven of fourteen layers with no
   visual anchor, one undifferentiated plate for four layer classes — is
   blocked behind this exact fence, not behind any open question the owner
   needs to think about. Nobody but Jerry can clear it, and every wave that
   passes without it clearing accumulates more work stacked behind the same
   gate. It is also the cheapest of the three in elapsed effort: placing one
   approved figure in a Word template and printing it is an afternoon, not a
   review cycle.
2. **The practitioner walk second**, because it resolves the questions that
   are genuinely subjective and that no measurement in this pipeline settles
   — the frozen-column cap (62vw vs. 45vw vs. a different narrow-viewport
   pattern entirely, now measured three ways and still undecided), and
   whether seven prose-only layer rows read as honest disclosure or as a
   wall of near-identical text on a second visit. These are real, they are
   recorded (not fixed, correctly, per §2.6's scope discipline) across three
   waves now, and a domain expert's fifteen minutes settles them faster than
   another round of measurement would.
3. **The PRD amendment last**, because it should record what the other two
   settle rather than anticipate it. If the print test surfaces a defect in
   the figure grammar itself (G-37c's own history — LibreOffice silently
   failing on a `.docx` nothing else caught — is exactly the kind of thing a
   print test finds that no other check does), or the practitioner walk
   decides the layer-manager pattern needs a different narrow-viewport
   treatment, the PRD amendment is the document that should say so once,
   correctly, rather than being written now and revised again after either
   of the other two lands. Writing it first risks the exact "typed claim
   beside a fact that later moved" shape this whole audit exists to catch —
   applied to the PRD itself.
