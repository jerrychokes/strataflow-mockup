# The second practitioner walk — the pack

**Yours to run.** D13 scheduled it after Wave 23, and Wave 23 has closed. This
is the pack: who, what to show, in what order, what to ask, and what an answer
changes. Written 3 September 2026 by the same hand that built the thing being
walked, which is the reason §12's gate exists and the reason this pack is
structured to make disagreement cheap.

Its evidence base is `2026-09-03-walk-evidence.md`, re-measured and now
reproducible (`python3 audits/walk-evidence.py 3112977 HEAD`).

## What this walk is for, in one sentence

**Not** *"are your corrections implemented"* — three of the four screens she
reviewed retain a tenth or less of the words she read, so that question has
almost no surface left to land on. **This walk asks whether the last five waves
built the right things**, and every one of them is unvalidated by anybody.

## Who

Somebody who **submits groundwater monitoring reports to a regulator for a
living** — a consultant hydrogeologist or an environmental scientist at a mining
or energy operator in WA, SA or Qld. Not a GIS analyst, not a data engineer, not
a software reviewer. The one qualification that matters is that a bad figure or
a missed notification has cost them personally.

If it is the same practitioner as the first review, say at the start that this
is new work rather than her corrections re-shown, and show her the evidence
table. She will otherwise assume continuity that is not there.

## The order, and why

Ninety minutes. The order is deliberate: the object first, then what reads it,
then the two decisions that ride on her answer, then the four spent screens
last — because if time runs out, the last section is the one worth losing.

### 1 · The governed dataset — `#saved-views` (20 min)

**The keystone. 6.8% of the words she read are still there, and nobody has ever
looked at it.** Seven of the eleven vendor requirements hang off this object.

Show: a dataset's definition, its version, the live/frozen seam, the four
absences counted by kind, and a citation resolved at both ends.

Ask:
- *"Is this the object you'd reach for, or do you think in reports?"* — the
  whole design assumes the question is the durable thing and the report is
  downstream. If she thinks in reports, the object is wrong.
- *"A citation is bound to the version, not the rows. Is that what you'd want
  when a lab reissues a certificate six months later?"*
- *"Four kinds of absence, counted separately. Is that four, or more, or fewer?"*

### 2 · The explorer and the analysis object — `#explorer`, `#analysis` (15 min)

Show: composing a query across dimensions nobody anticipated, the population
readout, and the analysis that records what it was computed from.

Ask:
- *"What's the first question you'd ask of a round that this can't express?"*
  — the sharpest question in the pack, and the answer is a requirement.
- *"'Validated results only' empties this population, because the round sits at
  `screened`. Is that right, or is `screened` good enough to report on?"*

### 3 · The map as a workspace — `#map` (15 min)

Show: the layer manager with its governance columns, the symbology dials, the
four non-detect rules over the same six cells, and the spatial selection that
hands a population to the explorer.

Ask:
- *"Fourteen layers named, five drawn, seven counted with what each would need.
  Is that the honest set, and is the order of what's missing right?"*
- *"Two of forty-two governance answers carry a date. Does a layer's date matter
  to you, or is source enough?"*
- **The one that matters most:** *"Four substitution rules give four different
  maps of the same six cells. Which does your office use, and does the report
  say which?"*

### 4 · The two decisions taken without you (10 min)

- **D9** — a criterion generalises to a rule that produces a finding. *The most
  exposed answer in the set.* Show the criteria screen and ask whether a rule
  that fires is a finding or a suggestion.
- **D11** — `#crosstab` demoted to one representation of a population, losing
  its own filter bar. She read this screen. Ask whether losing the private
  filter bar costs her anything.

### 5 · The four spent screens (20 min, and the first to cut)

`#field-capture` (9.8%), `#lineage` (10.0%), `#narrative` (13.5%), `#qc`
(33.9%). Present as **new work**, not as her corrections.

Ask on `#field-capture`: *"Seven dispositions, and dry is one of them. Is that
list closed and is it the right seven?"*

### 6 · The figure grammar (10 min, only if the print test has run)

**Do not run this section on the current grammar.** The pre-print measurement in
the app repo (`docs/design/pre-print-measurement.md`) found that gridlines print
at 1% ink and the commonest state mark vanishes on a photocopy. Asking a
practitioner to judge plates with those faults wastes her time on defects
already known. Fix the grammar, re-approve, print, *then* ask.

## What to write down

For each answer: **what she said, whether it contradicts something drawn, and
what would have to change.** Not a tick. The catalogue's whole discipline is
that a disagreement is drawn rather than resolved, and a walk that produces
agreement produced nothing.

## The three questions to ask if there are only three

1. *"What's the first question you'd ask of a round that this can't express?"*
2. *"Four substitution rules, four maps, same six cells. Which does your office
   use, and does the report say which?"*
3. *"Is the dataset the object you'd reach for, or do you think in reports?"*

## What this pack cannot do, and the reason it is written this way

**I built the thing being walked.** Every question above is one I chose, which
means the questions I did not think to ask are invisible to it — and those are
the ones a first look is actually for. Two mitigations, and neither is
sufficient: the questions are open rather than yes/no, and the sections are
ordered so the unvalidated work is seen while attention is freshest.

The honest instruction is: **let her go off the pack.** If she spends forty
minutes on one screen that is not in this list, that is a better outcome than
completing the agenda.
