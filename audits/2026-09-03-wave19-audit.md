## Findings

### W19-A-1 · P2 — the citation check can never clear for a water-level figure, so correcting the defect it found would leave it still reporting a disagreement

- **Evidence (reproduce):** `citationsOf` computes
  `agrees: outside.length === 0 && analytes.length > 0`. Correcting the drawn defect —
  setting the item title to `"Groundwater elevation — MW05, MW07"`, exactly what the dataset
  selects — leaves `citationFinding.disagreeing === 1`, with the row now reading
  `outside: []` and the sole remaining ground `subject: "no analyte in the dictionary — the
  item draws water levels"`. A hydrograph names no analyte in the dictionary by nature, so
  the second conjunct can never hold for it.
- **Affected criterion:** not an acceptance criterion — the wave's own added check.
- **Expected:** a citation that names exactly what its dataset selects agrees, and the
  register's count falls.
- **Observed:** it stays at "1 of 2 disagree" with nothing outside the definition. The
  rendered sentence would become *"…and the item's subject is no analyte in the dictionary"*
  standing alone as the reason a correct citation disagrees.
- **Why only P2:** today's drawn claim is **true** — MW01A genuinely is outside, the row
  states both grounds explicitly, and the `subject` field names the structural one rather
  than hiding it. The failure is latent, and it fires on the obvious correction rather than
  on the current record. Nothing on a screen is false today.
- **Recommended correction:** make the analyte limb conditional on the item having a
  chemical subject at all — an item that draws water levels should be checked on locations
  and period, and `agrees` should not require an analyte it cannot have. Then a corrected
  citation clears, and the count means what a reader takes it to mean.
