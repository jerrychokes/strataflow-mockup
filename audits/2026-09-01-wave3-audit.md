## Findings

### W3-A-1 · P3 — The registers the cascade lands on carry no trace that anything is staged against them

- **Evidence:** measured — `#exceedances`, `#tarp` and `#supersession` contain no
  occurrence of "staged", "amendment" or "PAS2026-01884" (probe output), while
  `#snapshot` ("3 staged"), `#report-figures` ("stale — the staged amendment reaches two
  of its four series") and `#signoff` ("3 staged changes under a signed snapshot") each
  flag it. J4's steps "Supersession comparison" and "Changed exceedances" link a reader
  from the cascade to screens holding no trace of the case they just read; the
  supersession register shows only the absorbed PAS2026-04417 amendment.
- **Assessment:** the *values* are right everywhere (that is the staged model, and the
  seed states it), so this is unevenness rather than error: three downstream surfaces
  know what is staged against them and three do not. A practitioner reading the
  exceedance register during a pending amendment has no signal that two of its rows have
  amended values awaiting a decision.
- **Recommended correction:** the one-line staged notice the snapshot table already uses
  (or report-figures' stale-tag idiom) on the three silent registers — or a recorded
  decision that they stay silent, so the unevenness is chosen rather than accreted.

### W3-A-2 · P3 — The body-prose state-claim guard cannot see the phrasing `#ecoc` uses

- **Evidence:** the auditor's text-node walk flags `#ecoc`: "Proposed. The PRD names no
  chain-of-custody surface, and there is no route for one." This **agrees** with the
  register (`now: 'proposed'`) today. But `build.mjs`'s guard bans only
  `/no route (?:exists|imports|renders)/` and the fixed phrase from W1-A-2, so "there is
  no route for one" is invisible to it — if the product ships an eCOC route and the
  register flips, this sentence rots exactly the way W1-A-2 did, with the guard green.
- **Recommended correction:** widen the pattern (e.g. `/there is no route/`) or compute
  the sentence from the register state, as the coverage sentence now is.
